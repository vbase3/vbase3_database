Companion Animal Antibody Architecture

The Veterinary Monoclonal Antibody Revolution:

Companion animal therapeutics has undergone a rapid paradigm shift with the advent of "caninized" monoclonal antibodies. Rather than using small molecules with non-specific organ toxicity, veterinary medicine now utilizes high-affinity caninized antibodies engineered to match the natural canine immunoglobulin architecture.

Key Structural & Immunogenetic Hallmarks in VBASE3:

  • Inverted Light Chain Partitioning (~90% Lambda Dominance): Whereas humans exhibit ~60% Kappa and mice >95% Kappa, dogs display an inverted repertoire dominated by the Lambda light chain (~90% IGL vs. ~10% IGK). Therapeutic caninization campaigns must account for this structural preference when selecting light chain pairings.
  • Restricted Heavy Chain Families: The canine heavy chain repertoire is predominantly composed of Clan III-related IGHV genes that exhibit high homology to human IGHV3, greatly simplifying human-to-canine antibody conversion.
  • Strict IMGT Synthesis Boundaries: Canonical synthesis endpoints (TVSS for heavy, TVL for lambda, and VEIK/LEIK for kappa) ensure zero constant region bleed-through in recombinant veterinary production.
  • Repertoire-Confirmed Class I Functional Universe: All 29 canine V-genes in this analyzer represent confirmed functional segments with rearranged physiological evidence, trimmed strictly to Cys104.
